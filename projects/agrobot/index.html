01THE PROBLEM

What needed solving?

Soil conditions are difficult to track remotely. AgroBot explores a simple way to sense moisture, signal unsafe conditions locally, and bring readings to a dashboard.

02MY CONTRIBUTION

Where I came in.

I designed and built a compact prototype around a NodeMCU V3, resistive soil-moisture sensor, and piezoelectric buzzer. HTTP telemetry pairs current values with a seven-day trend view.

03KEY DECISIONS

The thinking behind it.

01

Simplify the board architecture

Moving from Arduino Uno plus a separate ESP8266 to NodeMCU combines sensing and Wi-Fi on one board.

02

Keep an offline feedback path

The buzzer signals dry or waterlogged conditions independently of the network.

03

Show change over time

A current reading gives immediate context. Historical readings show how conditions evolve.

04OUTCOME & NEXT STEPS

Built. Learned.
Still improving.

Built as a Capstone-I prototype at IIT Patna. My résumé documents hardware, enclosure, telemetry, and dashboard work.

Where it stands today

The public README marks server/database integration and the final end-to-end test as pending. No measured farm deployment or water-saving result is claimed.

What comes next

Validate the complete data path and compare sensor behaviour across soil conditions before field deployment.
